The US is a **non-designated (non-rabies-free) region** under Japan's classification system, which triggers the most demanding import regime: an 8-step sequence involving microchipping, two rabies vaccinations, an antibody titer test at a MAFF-approved lab, a mandatory **180-day waiting period**, and government-endorsed health certificates. Done correctly, on-arrival quarantine at Narita clears in under 12 hours. Done with any error — including a step performed out of order — the dog can be detained for **up to 180 days at the owner's expense**, or returned.

The entire process takes a minimum of **~7 months from scratch**. Start as early as possible.

## Regulatory Overview

Two government agencies must be satisfied:

| Side | Agency | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Japan (import) | Animal Quarantine Service (AQS), under MAFF | Reviews advance notification, issues "Approval of Import Inspection," conducts arrival inspection |
| US (export) | USDA APHIS Veterinary Services | Endorses the health certificate (Form AC) prepared by a USDA-accredited vet |

The linchpin document is the **government-endorsed health certificate (Form AC)**, which records the microchip, both rabies vaccinations, the antibody test result, and the pre-export clinical exam. A USDA-accredited vet prepares it; APHIS endorses it.

> ⚠️ The APHIS endorsement process (including how Form AC is submitted and what you physically receive) is a US-side requirement not covered by the MAFF guide. Confirm the exact workflow with your USDA-accredited vet and APHIS before departure.

## The 8-Step Process

The legal step order is irreversible. A step performed out of sequence is not accepted and forces a restart from that point.

### Step 1 — ISO Microchip

- Must be an **ISO 11784/11785** chip: 15-digit numeric code.
- **Must be implanted before or on the same day as the first rabies vaccination.** A vaccination administered before the chip is invalid.
- Non-ISO chips: you must supply a compatible reader at the port of entry (contact AQS in advance).
- **Flagged chip series:** MAFF does not currently accept chips starting with "900 202." Several other "900"-series numbers (e.g. 900113, 900115, 900128, 900201, 900215, 900217, 900263) have documented duplication problems; a duplicate may require re-chipping and restarting all procedures. Submit the advance notification promptly once timing is decided so AQS can flag any issue early.
- The microchip number must appear **identically** on the titer report, the Form AC, the advance notification, and the import application. A mismatch across any of these triggers detention.

### Step 2 — Two Rabies Vaccinations

Both vaccinations must be administered **after** the microchip is implanted.

- **First vaccination:** the dog must be **at least 91 days old** (birth = day 0). Vaccination at 84–90 days is invalid under Japanese rules even if permitted in the US.
- **Second vaccination:** at least **30 days after the first** (first dose = day 0), and within the effective period (duration of immunity) of the first.
- **Accepted vaccine types only:** inactivated (killed) virus or recombinant/modified vaccine. **Live-virus and RNA rabies vaccines are not accepted.**
- If the vaccine's effective period would expire before arrival, a booster must be given within the effective period. A booster given after expiry counts as a new "first" vaccination and restarts the sequence.
- Confirm the product name, manufacturer, lot number, and effective period are documented for each dose — all must appear on Form AC.

> **If vaccinations were done at a different clinic from the one completing Form AC:** confirm in writing that the ISO microchip was implanted before or on the same day as the first rabies shot. A microchip-after-vaccination sequence error is the most common cause of a full restart. See the MAFF "Certain Conditions" exception below if this applies.

### Step 3 — Rabies Antibody Test (FAVN/RFFIT)

- Blood must be drawn **after the second rabies vaccination** (same day as the second shot is permitted).
- Must be tested at a **MAFF-designated laboratory**. For US civilians the only option is **Kansas State University (KSU) Rabies Laboratory** (2005 Research Park Circle, Manhattan, KS 66502; +1-785-532-4483). The DoD lab at Fort Sam Houston is also MAFF-approved but restricted to US military personnel.
- Accepted test methods: **FAVN** (fluorescent antibody virus neutralization) or **RFFIT** (rapid fluorescent focus inhibition test). Micro-RFFIT is not accepted.
- Result must be **≥ 0.5 IU/ml**. Below that, revaccinate and retest.
- The result is **valid for 2 years** from the blood-draw date.
- The **original** lab report must be presented at the arrival inspection.
- KSU logistics: the sample is submitted through the vet clinic (not directly by the owner). Submit 1–2 mL of clear serum in a tube **labeled with the microchip number** — KSU cancels unlabeled submissions with no exceptions. Turnaround averages **2–3 weeks, up to 4 weeks**; no expedited service is available. Plan accordingly.

### Step 4 — 180-Day Waiting Period

- The dog must arrive in Japan **on or after 180 days from the blood-draw date** (draw date = day 0).
- Arrival must also fall within both the **titer's 2-year validity** and the **vaccine's effective period**.
- If even one day short, AQS detains the dog for the shortfall period.
- **Computing your deadline:** count back 180 days from your planned arrival date — the blood draw must be on or before that date. Verify this date before booking flights.

### Step 5 — Advance Notification to AQS (≥40 Days Before Arrival)

- File the "Notification of Import" with the AQS office for the port of entry **not less than 40 days before arrival**. A late notification is not accepted in principle.
- Submit via **NACCS** (online system) or by email/fax using the dog notification form.
- AQS issues the **"Approval of Import Inspection of Animals"** — print and save this for boarding and for the arrival inspection.
- Certain changes after filing are not accepted: moving the arrival date **earlier**, increasing the number of animals, or substituting a different animal. Changes must be filed as a "Modification on Notification."
- Narita AQS contacts: Terminal 1 — aqs.nrtr1@maff.go.jp / +81-476-32-6510; Terminal 2 — aqs.nrtr2@maff.go.jp / +81-476-34-2342 (hours 8:30–17:00 JST). Dedicated dog/cat travel line: +81-3-5708-7261 (9:00–17:00 JST).

### Step 6 — Pre-Export Clinical Exam (Within 10 Days of Departure)

- A **USDA-accredited vet** examines the dog and certifies it free of clinical signs of rabies and (for dogs) leptospirosis.
- The exam must occur **within 10 days before the boarding date**. Count back from your departure date to find the earliest it can be scheduled.

### Step 7 — Health Certificate (Form AC)

Steps 6 and 7 happen at the same vet appointment. After completing the clinical exam, the vet prepares Form AC using the exam result combined with the microchip, vaccination, and titer records.

- Form AC records: the dog's individual info (including DOB), microchip number and implant date, rabies vaccination dates/effective period/vaccine type/product/manufacturer, antibody-test date/titer/designated lab, and the clinical exam result and date.
- MAFF describes Form AC as the **recommended** certificate form.
- No pencil, no erasable ink, no correction fluid or tape. Any deficiency triggers detention or return.
- **Strongly recommended:** email a draft of the completed Form AC to the Narita AQS office before obtaining the APHIS endorsement so they can catch errors before it is locked.
- The completed Form AC must be endorsed by **USDA APHIS** before travel. Confirm the endorsement process — including what you physically receive and need to carry — directly with your vet and APHIS.

### Step 8 — Import Inspection at Arrival

- Go to the **AQS counter before clearing customs or immigration**.
- AQS offices operate **8:30–17:00 JST**. A dog arriving outside these hours cannot be released until the following day. Book flights to arrive well before closing and factor in possible delays.
- If everything is in order, clearance is normally **under 12 hours** — often a few hours.
- Present: original endorsed Form AC, original KSU titer report, AQS Approval of Import Inspection, and the import inspection application (can be submitted via NACCS in advance or completed on arrival).

## Document Summary

| Document | Submitted in advance | Travels with the dog (originals) |
|---|---|---|
| Advance notification | ✅ ≥40 days before arrival | — |
| AQS "Approval of Import Inspection" | — | ✅ |
| APHIS-endorsed Form AC | Draft for AQS pre-check (recommended) | ✅ Original endorsed copy |
| KSU antibody-test report | Copy for AQS pre-check (optional) | ✅ Original |
| Import inspection application | Via NACCS or on arrival | ✅ |

Keep copies of everything. The import quarantine certificate issued at arrival cannot be reissued — keep it (required if you ever export the dog again).

## After Arrival

- Register the imported dog with your municipality within **30 days** of import, using the import quarantine certificate.
- Japan's Rabies Prevention Law requires an **annual rabies vaccination** thereafter.

## Common Mistakes

- **Microchip implanted after the first vaccination.** The most common cause of a full restart. If vaccinations were done at a different clinic, verify the sequence in writing before proceeding.
- **Non-ISO or flagged microchip.** A non-ISO chip requires you to bring a compatible reader; a "900 202"-series chip is currently rejected; duplicated "900"-series numbers can force re-chipping and a full restart.
- **Wrong vaccine type.** Live-virus or RNA rabies vaccines are rejected. Confirm the product was inactivated or recombinant.
- **Titer at a non-designated lab or using a non-approved method.** Only MAFF-designated labs count (KSU for US civilians). Micro-RFFIT is not an approved method. Labs can lose MAFF designation — confirm the lab's current status on the MAFF designated-lab list before submitting.
- **Miscounting the 180 days.** The blood-draw date is day 0. Arriving even one day early means detention for the shortfall. Calculate carefully.
- **Missing the 40-day notification deadline.** A late notification is not accepted; you cannot move the arrival date earlier after filing.
- **Certificate defects.** Correction fluid, pencil, a missing vaccine detail, or a microchip-number mismatch triggers detention or return. Pre-check the draft with AQS before endorsement.
- **Certificate and titer expiry overlap.** Arrival must be within the vaccine's effective period **and** the titer's 2-year validity simultaneously.
- **AQS office hours / delayed flights.** An arrival outside 8:30–17:00 JST means the dog cannot be released until the next day. Build buffer into the itinerary.

## MAFF "Certain Conditions" Exception

If the first rabies vaccination was administered before the microchip was implanted, all is not automatically lost. MAFF allows a narrow exception provided strict conditions are met — contact AQS before assuming this applies.

The conditions:
- The first vaccination was given before the microchip was implanted.
- The dog was at least 91 days old at the time of the first vaccination.
- At least 30 days have passed since the first vaccination, and it is still within that vaccine's effective period.

Two pathways after the chip is implanted:

| Pathway | Sequence |
|---|---|
| One antibody test | Vaccination ① → Chip → [Blood draw + Vaccination ② on the same day] → 180-day wait |
| Two antibody tests | Vaccination ① → Chip → Blood draw ① → Vaccination ② → Blood draw ② → 180-day wait |

In both cases: only inactivated/recombinant vaccines count, the titer must be ≥0.5 IU/ml, and all vaccination and titer details must appear on the government-endorsed certificate. The 180-day clock runs from the date of the final blood draw.

## Considerations for Senior Dogs

Japan does not restrict entry based on a dog's age. For elderly dogs, a pre-travel veterinary fitness assessment (heart, lungs, kidneys, liver, hydration) beyond the standard health certificate exam is advisable. Sedation for air travel is generally discouraged — it can impair breathing and temperature regulation at altitude (per AVMA guidance). In-cabin travel is lower stress than cargo where the airline and dog's size permit.
